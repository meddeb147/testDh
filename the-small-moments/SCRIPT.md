Most couples don't break up because of one big fight. They break up over moments so small… nobody notices.

She says, "Look at that sunset." He doesn't look up from his phone. He says, "I had the weirdest day." She says, "That's nice"… and keeps typing. It feels like nothing. But it isn't nothing.

Psychologists call these bids. Any small attempt to connect. A comment. A joke. A touch. A random meme. Every bid gets one of three answers. You turn toward it. You ignore it. Or you shut it down.

One famous study followed newlywed couples for six years. The couples who stayed together turned toward each other's bids eighty-six percent of the time. The ones who divorced? Only thirty-three.

Not the big fights. Just whether you looked up… when they said, "Look at this."

Because every ignored bid quietly teaches one lesson: don't bother. So they share a little less. Then a little less. Until one day, you're two people on the same couch… scrolling in silence. Strangers who still know each other's passwords.

You don't need a loyalty test. You don't need to check their phone. Just watch what happens when they say, "Look at this." And when it's your turn… put the phone down. Look up. Say, "Show me."

Love isn't built in the big moments. It's built in the tiny ones you almost missed.

Send this to the person you'd always look up for. And follow for more.

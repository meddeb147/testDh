# Step 1: viral topic research and script writing

## Research (2–4 searches, run them in parallel)

1. `"<genre> TikTok trends <current year>"` or `"viral <genre> content Reels Shorts"`: what themes keep recurring.
2. The topic's substance: the psychology, history or real facts (e.g. "Gottman stonewalling research",
   "walkaway wife syndrome"). Real ideas make scripts feel credible and shareable.
3. Optional: what's already saturated, so you can find a fresh angle on a proven topic.

Trend pages are mostly marketing blogs. Treat their numbers as directional, don't quote statistics unless a
credible source states them, and never invent stats. List sources at the end of your reply.

### What makes a topic travel
Pick topics that tick at least 3:
- **Recognition:** "this is literally me / my partner / my ex." It triggers tags and shares.
- **Reframe:** flips a common belief ("silence isn't peace, it's goodbye"; "he pulls away *because* he loves her").
- **Strong single emotion:** heartbreak, dread, relief, laughter. Not a mix.
- **Debate fuel:** both sides comment (men vs women, "red flag or not").
- **Visualizable:** concrete objects and actions you can draw (a sinking ship, a phone typing then deleting, a flatline).
- **Series potential:** part 1 / part 2 from the other side.

## Script formula (60–100 s, ≈150–240 words)

| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | A reframe, question or shocking statement. No intro, no "hey guys". |
| Tension | 3–15 s | Why the obvious explanation is wrong; the common mistake. |
| Story / mechanism | 15–60 s | Step-by-step escalation in short, concrete, visual lines. |
| Turn | ~60–75 s | The painful or surprising consequence (the "that's where…" moment). |
| Payoff | last 15 s | Actionable advice / chilling twist / punchline, then one memorable closing line. |
| CTA | last 3 s | "Send this to someone who needs to hear it. And follow for more." (adapt to genre) |

Writing rules:
- One idea per sentence, 4–12 words. Narrators pause at punctuation: use `…` for dramatic pauses and periods for beats.
- Lists of three ("See me. Hear me. Choose me.") and contrasts ("Not with her suitcase… but with her heart").
- Every 2–3 lines should be drawable as one picture. If you can't picture it, rewrite it.
- Write "he/she/you" consistently. Avoid names, brands, real people, and medical or legal claims.
- No ads or product/book mentions unless the user asks.
- Put the title-style label for the video in caps (e.g. "WHEN SHE GOES QUIET"). It shows at the top of every frame.

## Genre playbook

| Genre | Hook styles | Emotional arc | Ending | THEME / MOOD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relationships / romance | reframe of a behavior ("When a woman stops fighting…") | recognition → ache → hope | actionable advice + share CTA | `relationships` / `tender` |
| Horror / creepy | "If you hear ___ at 3am, don't ___." Rules, "true story" framing | calm → unease → dread → twist | chilling last line, then "follow for part 2" | `horror` / `dark` |
| Comedy | relatable exaggeration ("Every couple has this fight…"), POV setups | setup → escalation → punchline | callback joke + "tag the friend who…" | `comedy` / `playful` |
| Motivation / self-growth | hard truth ("Nobody is coming to save you") | pain → realization → resolve | one-line mantra + "save this" | `motivation` / `uplifting` |
| Mystery / dark facts | "This place has no ___…" open loop | curiosity → reveal → bigger question | unresolved hook for part 2 | `mystery` / `dark` |
| Facts / psychology | "Your brain does this every time you…" | surprise → explanation → so-what | practical takeaway + "follow for more" | `facts` / `uplifting` |
